# Recent market history — 2026-09-10

Game detail now displays a bookmaker-selectable table of saved pregame spread, total and moneyline observations. Acquisition time and per-market provider update time remain separate. The spread summary compares the first and latest eligible spread shown, reporting a net point change rather than a probability, expected return or betting recommendation. Missing markets stay missing. Repeated equal quotes remain distinct observations; unchanged prices between two captures do not prove that no intervening movement occurred.

This is explicitly a recent window: up to twelve verified captures from the current and previous two UTC dates. It is not the full private archive, an opening-line database, a certified closing line or CLV. After kickoff, eligible historical pregame observations remain visible within this window; the live comparison still expires. Exact team identities and kickoff instants must match, so a rescheduled event cannot silently reuse the earlier game's prices. Market freshness is assessed at acquisition, not at the later time a reader views history.

The server lists three bounded date prefixes and reads at most twelve compressed snapshots. Listings and reads are batched separately with five-second request limits. Incomplete listings are not treated as complete. Conflicting acquisition timestamp groups are removed before the cap, preventing truncation from hiding one member of an ambiguous group. SHA-256 covers the actual decompressed envelope bytes; regenerated path/content identity must also agree. Individual read failures produce an incomplete-history state instead of fabricated quotes. Only matchup-specific normalized observations reach client props; storage credentials and raw archive URLs remain server-side.

A shared six-hour cache across matchups is invalidated by the existing collection tag. This avoids per-game archive scans and adds no odds-provider requests. A fully populated refresh needs at most three list operations and twelve object reads. Five refreshes/day nominally add up to 1,800 object reads and 450 lists in thirty days, before cache misses, deployment/dev/preview activity or failures. This is a cost estimate, not a hard store-wide quota cap. The existing current-snapshot cache and collectors also consume storage operations.
