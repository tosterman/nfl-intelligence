# Fixed joint-score evaluation protocol

Evaluate both candidates on all 285 retained 2025 historical forecasts. These are reused development records, not an untouched holdout. Read the exact published forecast artifact; do not refit its point predictions. Fit distribution priors only from completed 2010–2023 games, excluding all 2024–2025 outcomes. Freeze both priors for the entire evaluation.

Use integer home/away score cells 0–100. Mirror historical team orientation. Candidate: observed joint-score counts plus 100 pseudogames drawn from the outer product of the empirical marginal score distribution with one count added per score. Reference: a discrete Gaussian grid using the mirrored historical score mean and covariance, retaining its observed overdispersion. Both priors use the same joint-score solver to match each published expected home and away score. Both use the same historical regular-season Beta(1,99) tie estimate; postseason tie mass is zero. No alternative grid, smoothing, reference, subset or seed will be selected after viewing results.

Primary measure: mean negative log probability of the actual joint final score. Secondary: margin and total negative log scores, discrete margin and total CRPS, and three-outcome win/tie/loss Brier. Report expected versus observed ties and maximum score-mean reconciliation error. Fail if an observed score lies outside the grid or any required probability is zero/nonfinite; do not clip probabilities or silently omit games. Compare primary scores with paired, whole-week percentile resampling (10,000 draws, seed20260910). Retain per-game evidence and all input/protocol/code fingerprints.

This evaluates two research distributions, not a settlement-ready simulation or the published continuous normal model. The finite grid, old overtime-rule mix, fixed historical shape, source revisions and reused model-development sample limit interpretation. Even a convincing primary improvement does not authorize production promotion: current-rule ties, tails, key-number and market-settlement calibration, prospective performance, and reconciliation with public intervals remain required. A non-improvement or interval spanning zero is insufficient for promotion. Do not revise this protocol after inspecting the scores.
